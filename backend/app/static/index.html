Understanding Map Colors & Radar Reflectivity (dBZ)

Radar reflectivity measures the concentration and size of precipitation particles in the atmosphere:

15 – 30 dBZ (Light to Moderate Rain) Stratiform rain or early developing shower. Minimal lightning risk.
35 – 45 dBZ (Heavy Convective Rain) Convective initiation. Updrafts carrying substantial moisture aloft.
50 – 58 dBZ (Severe Thunderstorm) Vigorous convective core, intense cloud-to-ground lightning, and gusty winds.
60+ dBZ (Violent Hail / Severe Supercell) Extremely dangerous storm core with high probability of large hail and microbursts.

Key Physical Indicators Explained

⚡ Schultz 2σ Lightning Jump

A sudden exponential surge in total lightning flash rate ($\Delta F / \sigma \ge 2.0$) that precedes severe downbursts and hail by 15 to 30 minutes.

🔥 CAPE (Updraft Energy)

Convective Available Potential Energy ($J/kg$). High CAPE ($>2000$) provides the thermodynamic fuel needed for explosive thunderstorm updrafts.

🛡️ CIN (Convective Inhibition)

The atmospheric "lid" or barrier. Low CIN ($<50\text{ J/kg}$) allows storms to trigger easily, while high CIN caps convection.

🛰️ Satellite Cloud Top Cooling

INSAT-3D measures cloud-top infrared temperature. Fast cooling ($\le -5^\circ\text{C} / 15\text{min}$) indicates rapidly climbing updrafts penetrating the tropopause.
