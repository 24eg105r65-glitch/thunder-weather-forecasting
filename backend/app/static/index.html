Understanding Map Colors & Radar Reflectivity (dBZ)

Radar reflectivity measures the concentration and size of precipitation particles in the atmosphere:

15 - 30 dBZ (Light to Moderate Rain) Stratiform rain or early developing shower. Minimal lightning risk.
35 - 45 dBZ (Heavy Convective Rain) Convective initiation. Updrafts carrying substantial moisture aloft.
50 - 58 dBZ (Severe Thunderstorm) Vigorous convective core, intense cloud-to-ground lightning, and gusty winds.
60+ dBZ (Violent Hail / Severe Supercell) Extremely dangerous storm core with high probability of large hail and microbursts.

Key Physical Indicators Explained

Schultz 2σ Lightning Jump

A sudden exponential surge in total lightning flash rate (dF/dt ≥ 2.0σ) that precedes severe downbursts and hail by 15 to 30 minutes.

CAPE (Updraft Energy)

Convective Available Potential Energy (J/kg). High CAPE (>2000 J/kg) provides the thermodynamic fuel needed for explosive thunderstorm updrafts.

CIN (Convective Inhibition)

The atmospheric "lid" or barrier. Low CIN (<50 J/kg) allows storms to trigger easily, while high CIN caps convection.

Satellite Cloud Top Cooling

INSAT-3D measures cloud-top infrared temperature. Fast cooling (≤ -5 °C / 15min) indicates rapidly climbing updrafts penetrating the tropopause.

Privacy Policy & Data Governance

Aerocast-AI is committed to full user privacy and data minimization. This platform operates as an open meteorological early-warning cockpit with zero monetization of personal data.

1. Zero Personal Data Tracking

We do not collect names, phone numbers, email addresses, IP logs, or personal identifiers. No third-party marketing scripts or behavioral tracking cookies are used.

2. Ephemeral Geolocation Processing

Searched locations and GPS coordinates are processed strictly in volatile memory to calculate distance to radar storm cells and fetch local nowcasts. Coordinates are never persisted in databases.

3. Public Meteorological Ingestion

Doppler radar reflectivity, INSAT-3D infrared cloud temperatures, and lightning strike data are gathered from open meteorological services (OpenWeatherMap, IMD, NOAA) for disaster risk reduction.

4. Client-Side Simulation & Soundings

Thermodynamic sounding analysis (Skew-T Log-P), time-series meteogram rendering, and What-If scenario simulations run locally in your web browser runtime.

5. NDMA / IMD Standard Compliance

Emergency advisories adhere to National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) and Common Alerting Protocol (CAP v1.2) standards for life safety.
